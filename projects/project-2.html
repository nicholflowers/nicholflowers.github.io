Race & Empathy in Childbirth Care

A mixed-methods research design proposal investigating racial disparities in how medical professionals respond to women in pain during childbirth.

Problem

Black women in the United States are roughly three times more likely to die from pregnancy-related causes than white women, and qualitative reports consistently describe medical professionals being less responsive to the pain of women of color during labor.

The goal was to develop a research proposal that demonstrates how a data science team would rigorously investigate this question. The central question: Does race impact how empathetically medical professionals respond to expressions of pain by a woman in labor? The methodological challenge was operationalizing "empathy" into something measurable across a sample while preserving its meaning.

Approach

We designed a convergent mixed-methods study combining four data streams:

  • Patient survey rating responsiveness on 1-5 scales across operationalized dimensions of empathy (acknowledgment of pain, suggestions for relief, speed of response to medication requests, verbal and physical comfort)
  • Support person survey capturing the same observations from a witness in the room, providing triangulation
  • Semi-structured interviews with both patients and support people, allowing themes to emerge that the survey couldn't anticipate
  • Medical professional interviews establishing the standard of care so any deviation could be identified

The sampling plan recruited equal numbers of white and non-white participants across socioeconomic strata. Validity safeguards included triangulation across data sources, member checking with participants, peer debriefing, and explicit attention to discrepant cases.

Results

This was a design proposal, not a completed study. The deliverable was the research framework itself: a study design rigorous enough to produce actionable evidence on whether racial disparities in empathy during childbirth exist and how they manifest.

The framework successfully operationalized empathy into measurable survey dimensions, established a sampling strategy that controls for socioeconomic confounds, and built in validity safeguards to ensure findings would hold up to scrutiny.
